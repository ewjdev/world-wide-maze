# Ages 4–6 starter material

This is an original, English-language early-math pilot, not a full curriculum, developmental assessment, or certified learning program. Age is a suggested starting audience. A child may need a simpler or more challenging experience independent of age. Six-year-old extension and literacy coverage remain open work.

## Content register

| Activity | Goal | Limitation / next extension |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One, two, three | Match three to a group | Change objects/positions; observe one-to-one counting |
| A handful of five | Count five objects | Vary arrangements; fixed-option repetition can become position memory |
| Which has more? | Compare two and four objects | Vary spacing; ask the child to explain |
| Meet the triangle | Three straight sides and corners | Add rotated and differently proportioned examples |
| What comes next? | Extend an AB pattern | Try movement/sound patterns to explore transfer |
| Find its family | Sort by shape despite color changes | Let the child invent another sorting rule |

Questions have one correct option; wrong choices offer a hint and unlimited retry. Every activity can be selected freely. Browser speech is opt-in, not required; a grown-up can read aloud. The app does not store answers or claim mastery, and geometry is distinguished by shape as well as color.

## Evidence informing the scope

Accessed September 26, 2026. These sources informed topic choice; none validates or endorses this implementation.

- [Head Start preschool mathematics](https://headstart.gov/school-readiness/article/math-preschool) describes counting/cardinality, comparison, patterns, and geometry. Its preschool scope is not evidence that this is a comprehensive ages 4–6 sequence.
- [NAEYC: Fun, Easy Ways to Play with Math at Home](https://www.naeyc.org/node/2631) offers family activities involving number, shape, pattern, and measurement.
- [NAEYC: Developmentally Appropriate Practice](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/position-statements/dap/statement-position) frames early learning around joyful, engaged experiences responsive to the child.

## First family/educator review

Have an early-childhood educator check mathematical wording, distractors, accessibility, and sequence. With a small group of consenting families, observe whether parents understand the goal, children can use the controls, hints help, and the same concept appears in a fresh off-screen activity. Record observed behavior separately from interpretation; participation and correct answers alone are not learning-outcome evidence.

Use the results to choose the next content increment: richer math variations for ages 5–6, early language activities, or a simpler starting path. Do not expand every subject and age band at once.
